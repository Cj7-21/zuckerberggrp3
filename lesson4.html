📖 Lesson 4 – Online Etiquette, Digital Citizenship & ICT Careers

Learning Objectives

  • Identify different ICT‑related careers.
  • Understand Digital Citizenship and Netiquette.
  • Learn the core rules of online courtesy.
  • Recognize types of cyberbullying and digital footprint.

ICT Sectors & Careers

Career Description
Programmer Creates and maintains computer programs and software using programming languages.
Web Developer Designs, builds, and maintains websites and web applications.
Graphic Designer Creates visual designs such as posters, logos, advertisements, and digital graphics.
Animator Creates moving images or visual effects for films, games, advertisements, and other media.
IT Support Specialist Helps users troubleshoot and solve computer, network, and technical problems.
Cybersecurity Specialist Protects computers, networks, systems, and data from cyberattacks and security threats.
Data Analyst Helps organizations organize, manage, and understand data so they can make better decisions.
E‑Commerce Specialist Manages the process of buying and selling goods and services over the Internet through digital devices and platforms.

Digital Citizenship

Digital Citizenship means using technology responsibly, respectfully, safely, and appropriately.

Netiquette

Netiquette = Network + Etiquette. It refers to the proper manner in an online community environment.

Core Rules of Online Courtesy

  • Remember the Human: There is a real person behind the screen.
  • Follow Real‑Life Standards: Honesty, respect, responsibility, and appropriate behavior apply online too.
  • Share Expert Knowledge: If you know something useful, share it.
  • Respect Privacy: Not everything you see is yours to share.
  • Don’t Abuse Your Power: Just because you CAN doesn’t mean you SHOULD.
  • Forgive Mistakes: Nobody is perfect online.

Cyberbullying

Cyberbullying includes hurtful messages and harmful actions done online.

Type Description
Harassment Repeatedly sending unwanted, hurtful, threatening, or abusive messages.
Denigration Spreading false, cruel, or damaging information about someone online.
Outing Sharing someone’s private or embarrassing information without permission.
Impersonation Pretending to be another person online, especially to cause harm or embarrassment.
Exclusion Deliberately leaving someone out of an online group or activity to hurt or humiliate them.
Cyberstalking Repeatedly monitoring, contacting, threatening, or intimidating someone through digital means.

Digital Footprint

Digital Footprint is the trail of information and activity you leave when using digital technologies and the Internet.

  • Social media posts
  • Online platform accounts
  • Reviews & ratings
  • Messaging history
  • Cookies & trackers
  • IP addresses & metadata
  • App data

Process: Post → Share → Screenshot → Save
